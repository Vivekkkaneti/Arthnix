Understand the Metric

One formula.
More business context.

Revenue per employee provides a high-level view of revenue relative to the size of the workforce. It can be useful for tracking changes over time, provided the figures are calculated consistently.

A higher result does not automatically mean a company is more efficient. Industry, pricing, capital intensity, outsourcing, profit margins, and workforce structure can all influence the figure.

01

Identify total revenue

Use the revenue figure for a clearly defined period, such as a month, quarter, or financial year. Use the same accounting basis when comparing one period with another.

Example: Annual revenue = ₹1,20,00,000
02

Determine average employee count

If headcount changes during the period, average headcount can be more representative than using only the starting or ending number. Apply a consistent method to all periods being compared.

Simple estimate: (Beginning headcount + ending headcount) ÷ 2
03

Divide revenue by average headcount

Divide the period's revenue by the average number of employees for that period. The resulting figure represents revenue per average employee, not an employee's personal sales or output.

Formula: Revenue per employee = Total revenue ÷ Average employee count
04

Compare like with like

When reviewing trends, use comparable periods, revenue definitions, workforce definitions, and currencies. Annualize figures carefully if you need to compare periods of different lengths.

Period change: ((Current result − Previous result) ÷ Previous result) × 100

Suppose a business reports ₹1.2 crore in annual revenue and an average workforce of 40 employees. The resulting revenue-per-employee figure can be calculated as follows.

Frequently Asked Questions

Revenue per employee, explained.

Learn how to interpret the metric, compare reporting periods, and understand its limitations.

What is revenue per employee?
Revenue per employee is a business metric calculated by dividing total revenue for a defined period by the average number of employees during that period. It provides a high-level view of revenue relative to workforce size.
How do you calculate revenue per employee?
Divide the total revenue for the period by the average employee count for the same period. For example, ₹1,20,00,000 in annual revenue divided by 40 average employees equals ₹3,00,000 per employee per year.
Should I use total or net revenue?
Use the revenue definition that best matches your reporting purpose and apply it consistently. Net revenue may exclude items such as discounts, returns, or allowances depending on your accounting practices. Confirm the appropriate definition with your finance team.
How should I calculate average employee count?
If the workforce changes during a period, an average based on regular headcount measurements can be more representative than a single date's headcount. A simple estimate is the average of beginning and ending headcount. More frequent measurements may give a better estimate when workforce changes are significant.
Is a higher revenue per employee always better?
Not necessarily. The metric varies by industry, pricing, outsourcing, business model, and capital intensity. A company may generate more revenue per employee but have lower profit margins or higher costs. Review it alongside profitability, service quality, and other relevant business measures.
Can I compare companies using this metric?
Comparisons can be informative when companies have similar business models, reporting periods, accounting definitions, and workforce structures. Interpret comparisons cautiously if one company outsources more work, relies on contractors, or operates in a different industry.
Does revenue per employee measure employee productivity?
It is a company-level financial ratio, not a direct measure of an individual's productivity. Revenue can be influenced by prices, demand, technology, capital, business strategy, and many other factors beyond an employee's control.
Is this calculator suitable for financial reporting?
It is intended for estimation and business analysis. Verify the underlying revenue and headcount figures before using the result in financial reports, investor materials, or management decisions. This calculator does not replace professional accounting advice.
