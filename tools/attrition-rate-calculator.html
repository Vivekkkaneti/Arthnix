The Method

How the calculation works.

Attrition rate measures employee exits relative to a defined workforce size during a specified period. The denominator you choose can affect the resulting percentage.

01

Find average headcount

The standard method in this calculator averages the number of employees at the beginning and end of the reporting period.

(Opening headcount + Closing headcount) ÷ 2
02

Count employee exits

Count employees who left during the selected period. Apply the same definition of an exit throughout the report.

Total exits during the reporting period
03

Calculate the percentage

Divide the exits by the selected headcount denominator and multiply the result by 100 to obtain the attrition percentage.

(Employee exits ÷ Selected headcount) × 100
Practical Guidance

Use attrition data with context.

A percentage is a useful starting point, but it does not explain why employees leave or whether the exits are expected.

✓

Keep reporting periods consistent

Compare like-for-like monthly, quarterly, or annual periods. Clearly label the reporting period and calculation method.

✓

Separate voluntary and involuntary exits

Where possible, review resignations separately from dismissals, retirements, redundancies, and other departures.

✓

Review teams and roles separately

Organization-wide figures can hide different patterns across teams, locations, job families, and tenure groups.

✓

Investigate causes before drawing conclusions

Combine attrition data with exit interviews, employee feedback, hiring patterns, and operational context.

Calculation assumptions & disclaimer

This calculator provides a general workforce attrition estimate based on the figures and denominator method you select. Organizations may define attrition, employee exits, and headcount differently. For example, some reports exclude internal transfers, contractors, or certain categories of separation. This tool does not determine the reasons for employee departures, predict future turnover, or establish whether a rate is good or bad. Confirm your organization's HR reporting definitions before using results for business decisions.
