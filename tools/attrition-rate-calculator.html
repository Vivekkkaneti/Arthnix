How It Works

A simple formula. A clearer picture.

Attrition becomes easier to understand when employee exits are considered alongside the average size of your workforce.

STEP 01

Enter workforce figures

Add the number of employees at the start and end of the period, total exits, new hires, and voluntary exits if known.

STEP 02

Calculate average headcount

The calculator adds starting and ending headcount, then divides the total by two to estimate average headcount.

STEP 03

Review your attrition rate

Total exits are divided by average headcount and multiplied by 100 to produce the percentage for the selected period.

HR Fundamentals

Understand what your numbers mean.

Attrition is one workforce indicator. Interpreting it alongside hiring, retention, employee feedback, and organizational changes provides a more complete picture.

What is employee attrition rate?

Employee attrition rate measures the proportion of the average workforce that leaves an organization during a defined period. The exact definition may vary by company and reporting policy.

Attrition rate vs. employee turnover

These terms are often used interchangeably, but some HR teams distinguish attrition from turnover based on whether departing employees are replaced. Confirm the definition used in your organization's reports before comparing results.

Voluntary and involuntary exits

Voluntary exits are employee-initiated departures, such as resignations. Involuntary exits are employer-initiated departures, such as dismissals or redundancies. Their treatment in HR reports depends on your reporting policy.

Why compare periods consistently?

Comparing like-for-like periods can help identify changes in workforce movement. Consider seasonality, organizational restructuring, workforce growth, and changes in headcount definitions before interpreting differences.

Does a lower rate always mean better retention?

Not necessarily. Attrition should be interpreted in context. The reasons for departures, critical-role vacancies, hiring patterns, employee experience, and business changes all matter. A single percentage cannot explain why employees leave.
